"Today's Office" represents an important turning point for me. It is a collection of seven tracks for solo piano, composed over a span of about three years. A period of profound change, during which music was momentarily set aside to reorganize various aspects of my life. I changed jobs, transitioning from a freelance musician to a public administration official, facing all the doubts and fears that would assail any artist confronted with such a choice.

If initially the fear was that my artistic path would suffer a drastic and definitive halt, reality turned out to be quite different. Music has finally returned to being what it once was: an intimate need to create something that would make me happy, help me grow as a human being, and above all, help me describe what I felt.

I wanted to create an artwork for each track using always the same tool: a cold office scanner. Because, in the fear of a coldness I thought would come from working behind a desk, I sought out color; and in my realization as an adult, I rediscovered the simplicity of little things.

This is the reason for the subtitle "seven portraits for solo piano". Because that is what this is: an attempt to portray sensations, moods, and memories that have presented themselves to me over this time. For this reason, the pieces are presented in chronological order of composition, almost as if each of them were a page of a travel diary, which I want to share with anyone willing to listen.

Mauro Spanò
