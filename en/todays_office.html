"Today's Office" represents much more than an album for me. It is a collection of seven tracks for solo piano, composed over a rather long period of about three years. A time of profound change, during which music was temporarily put aside to reorganize various aspects of my life. I changed jobs, transitioning from a freelance musician to a public administration official, facing all the doubts and fears that would assail any artist making such a choice.

If initially the fear was that my artistic path would suffer a drastic and definitive halt, reality turned out to be quite different. Music finally returned to being what it once was: an intimate need to create something that made me happy, that made me grow as a human being and, above all, helped me describe what I had inside.

This is the reason for the subtitle "seven portraits for solo piano". Because this is what it's about: the attempt to give voice to a feeling, a state of mind, or simply a memory that lightened or weighed down my heart.

I wanted to create an artwork for each track using always the same tool: a cold office scanner. Because, in the fear of a coldness that I worried would come from working behind a desk, I looked for color; and in my realization as an adult, I rediscovered the simplicity of little things.

Each of these tracks is a page from a secret diary, which I share with you hoping to leave you with something.

Mauro Spanò
